Steps to miles: how far is 10,000 steps?

10,000 steps ≈ 4.4 miles (7 km) for someone 5′7″ (170 cm) with a stride of about 28 inches, which takes roughly 1 h 30 to 1 h 45 of walking. Taller people cover more ground: from about 4 miles at 5′1″ to 4.8 miles at 6′1″.

How the calculation works

Distance is simply steps × stride length. Stride length is hard to eyeball, so it is estimated from height: about 41% of how tall you are. At 5′7″ that is roughly 28 inches per step, so one mile takes about 2,300 steps.

For a more accurate number, walk a known distance (one lap of a 400 m track is a quarter mile), count your steps and divide.

Quick reference

  • 1 mile ≈ 2,000 to 2,400 steps depending on height.
  • 5,000 steps ≈ 2.2 miles, about 50 minutes of walking.
  • 10,000 steps ≈ 4.4 miles, 1 h 30 to 1 h 45 at a normal pace.
  • 20,000 steps ≈ 8.8 miles, more than 3 hours of walking.

In real life, those steps add up across the day: commuting, errands, stairs. That is why a monthly average is a better target than a round number every single day: a 6,000-step Tuesday is easily made up by a 14,000-step Saturday.

FAQ

How many miles is 10,000 steps for a woman?

For a woman 5′3″ to 5′5″, 10,000 steps is about 4.1 to 4.2 miles (6.6–6.8 km). The difference with men comes mostly from height, not sex.

How many steps are in a mile?

Between 2,000 and 2,400 steps for most adults, depending on height and pace. At 5′7″, count about 2,300.

How long does it take to walk 10,000 steps?

About 1 h 30 to 1 h 45 at a normal pace (100–110 steps per minute). In practice they are spread throughout the day.

Is this calculator accurate?

It is an estimate within about ±10%. Stride gets shorter uphill, in crowds or at a slow pace, and longer when you walk briskly.
